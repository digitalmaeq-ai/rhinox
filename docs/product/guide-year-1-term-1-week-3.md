# Guide: "Arrow Cards on the Grid" (Year 1, Term 1, Week 3)

**DRAFT. Not yet released.** The on-screen part of this lesson is not available to learners until a curriculum reviewer approves it (see `review-sheet-year-1-term-1-week-3.md`). The paper and floor-grid lesson below needs no screen and can be taught from the book today.

For teachers and parents. About 20 minutes.

## What your child learns
To write a precise set of arrow cards (an algorithm) that takes Ilana from START to FINISH on a grid, and to say what each card does. Words: algorithm, precise, forward, back, turn left, turn right, grid, square, start, finish.

## What you need (no screen, no robot)
- Arrow cards: ↑ forward one square, ↓ back one square (still facing the same way), ↶ turn left, ↷ turn right.
- A paper or chalk grid, 5×5 squares, and a bottle cap or paper Ilana.
- Children new to Think • Move • Code!: use a 4×4 grid and 3–4 cards (the support track).
- Groups of 3–4: planner, card-layer, robot, checker. Swap roles each round.

## The lesson, step by step
1. **Think.** Ilana is at START and the bread seller is at FINISH. Ask: which squares must she pass? Which way is she facing?
2. **Move.** Children act as the robot. One square for ↑, one step back for ↓, a turn on the spot for ↶ and ↷. A sticker on the left hand helps.
3. **Code.** Children lay 6–8 cards, with at least two turns, from START to FINISH. Before running, each child says what each card does. Then the robot follows the cards one at a time.
4. **Build (optional).** Make START and FINISH flags from card and a straw.
5. **Create.** Draw the path and the cards that made it.
6. **Check (teacher).** Watch a child write a working card line and say what each card does. Support track: 3–4 cards.

## About the screen version (when released)
- The child taps arrow cards to build a line, then presses **Run** and watches Ilana move step by step.
- The screen shows how many cards are used and the allowed range. It explains if Ilana bumps the edge or stops short.
- It only checks the run on the screen. **It cannot tell whether your child understood each card.** That is for you to observe.
- It never says "passed". The official check is you watching.
- Nothing is saved. Progress is lost when the page is closed.

## What the adult should do
- Ask "what does this card do?" before pressing Run.
- Let the child find mistakes by watching the steps, then change one card.

## What the adult should avoid
- Do not place the cards for the child.
- Do not record the check as done unless you watched them explain their cards.

## If your child gets stuck
Start with the 4×4 grid and 3 cards. Count squares aloud. Walk the route first, then lay the cards.

## Privacy
Do not type a child's name or details into any page. Nothing is sent or stored.
